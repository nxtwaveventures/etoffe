The risk is real

2026 is the year label claims got enforced.

These actions were reported this year. Many of the brands named are large companies with legal teams, and they still had to change their labels.

June 2026
Notices to more than a dozen brands

Companies including Emami Healthy & Tasty, Neuherbs, Troovy and The Health Factory were asked to explain terms such as healthy, organic and zero maida.

July 2026
"Energy drink" notices to six companies

Red Bull, PepsiCo India, Reliance Consumer Products and others were given 90 days to stop using the term for certain caffeinated drinks.

3 Aug 2026
Order against "100% Pure" and "100% Natural" claims

FSSAI ordered Dabur to stop selling food products carrying these claims, calling them unverifiable. The Delhi High Court stayed the order on procedural grounds while it hears the case.

18 Aug 2026
Six brands change their labels

Dabur India, Ferns N Petals, Lotte India and others changed listings, packaging and marketing claims after show-cause notices.

Now
FSSAI is testing AI to scan online listings

Reports say the system would track labels, ingredients and claims on e-commerce platforms, so problems can be flagged before any inspection.

What keeps founders up

The pressure comes from every side.

A risky claim is no longer just a legal question. It affects your sales, your funding and your reputation.

Regulator

Notices and orders

Show-cause notices give brands days to respond, and a single order can make a claim unusable across your whole range.

Public

Influencer call-outs

Label-reading creators review packs in front of millions of followers. One viral video can undo years of brand building.

Investors

Due diligence

Investors now check regulatory readiness before funding food and wellness startups, including claims in ads and influencer scripts.

Platforms

Weak listings

Missing ingredients, allergens or nutrition on Blinkit, Zepto and Amazon make products harder to find and easier to question.

Shoppers

Lost trust

Health-conscious buyers, including India's 101 million people with diabetes, read labels closely and switch brands when claims don't add up.

Product

No clear next step

When a claim fails, teams rarely know whether to change the wording, the recipe, or both.

Rulebook · updated 8 Oct 2026

Checked against the latest norms.

Every product is checked against the rules in force today, rules already notified for the future, and orders and proposals that signal where enforcement is heading. When a rule changes, we update the rulebook and can re-check your range.

In forceLabelling & Display Regulations, 2020

Mandatory label details: ingredients, allergens, nutrition, veg / non-veg symbol, dates, FSSAI logo and licence number.

In forceAdvertising & Claims Regulations, 2018

Claims must be truthful, scientifically backed and not misleading. Nutrient claims must meet set thresholds, and disease-risk claims need prior approval.

In force · May 2026Product & Claim Approval portal

Health claims that need approval now go through FSSAI's single digital portal, with a technical dossier.

In force · 1 Jul 2026Coffee–chicory declaration

Coffee and chicory percentages must appear on the front of the pack.

Effective 1 Jul 2027Labelling First Amendment, 2026

New warnings for artificially sweetened products, small-pack logo rules and non-retail container marking. We flag what to change before reprinting.

Under court review"100%" claims

FSSAI's 2024 juice directive and its August 2026 order against "100% Pure / Natural" claims. We treat these claims as high risk and track the case.

Notices issued · 2026"Healthy", "organic", "zero maida", "energy drink"

Terms questioned in FSSAI notices this year. We check whether your recipe and certification support them.

ProposedFront-of-pack warning label

FSSAI has proposed a red warning label for foods high in sugar, salt or fat, based on the Dietary Guidelines for Indians, 2024. We show which products would carry it.
